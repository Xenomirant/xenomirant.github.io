## what i do

I am a PhD student in AI at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen. My supervisor is Zhi-Quan Tom Luo. I work on two questions: which parts of a model are active for a given input, and how to train parameters that have tensor structure so that the optimizer respects it. Part-time, I am a MARS fellow with the Cambridge AI Safety Hub. There I study how language models behave when text is tokenized in unusual ways.

## how i got here

My first degree was in theoretical linguistics, with fieldwork on Itelmen, Adyghe and Kildin Sami. A master's in computational linguistics and mathematics turned me to the geometry of transformers. I studied how representations spread out, how they lose rank, and how much attention can amplify a change in its input. I then spent a year in HSE's matrix and tensor methods lab, working on optimizers. Before the PhD I evaluated language models in a bank's model risk team. That job left me careful about what benchmarks measure.

## interests

I think most unlearning methods hide knowledge rather than remove it. I think many membership-inference benchmarks, which test whether a sample was in the training data, mostly measure distribution shift. I think optimizer results depend far more on tuning than papers admit.

## elsewhere

I speak Finnish at B2 and a little Chinese. I photograph city lights at night. I grew up in Russia and sometimes write in Russian.
