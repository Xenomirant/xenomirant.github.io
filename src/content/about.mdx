## what i do

I am a PhD student in AI at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, where I work with Zhi-Quan Tom Luo on structure in neural computation: context-dependent sparse bases, tensor-shaped parameters, and the optimizers that ought to respect them. Part-time, I do AI safety research with a group in Cambridge, most recently as a MARS fellow studying how language models survive, and exploit, being tokenised wrongly.

## how i got here

Sideways. My first degree was in theoretical linguistics, with fieldwork on Itelmen, Adyghe and Kildin Sami, languages whose grammars do not fit a transformer's intuitions, which is partly why I started asking what fits in a transformer at all. A master's in computational linguistics and mathematics turned the question into geometry: isotropy, rank collapse, Lipschitz bounds on attention. A year in HSE's matrix and tensor methods lab turned the geometry into optimizers. A stint doing LLM evaluations in model risk at a bank turned all of it into a lasting suspicion of benchmarks.

## priors

I think writing and judgment are the two skills agents erode fastest, so I keep a blog to practise both. I think most unlearning is suppression, most membership-inference benchmarks measure distribution shift, and most optimizer papers are a hundred thousand learning-rate sweeps with a story attached, including mine. I think the university is a casino, and I am still at the table. I am transparent about machine co-authorship when it happens; the hackathon report on this site credits the model that wrote it.

## elsewhere
