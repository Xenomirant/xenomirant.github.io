This is a personal site. It is mostly a blog, with a CV attached.

What goes here: notes on the things I work on. Parameters with tensor structure and the optimizers that train them. Tokenization, and what happens to a language model when its input is split in unusual ways. Unlearning, and whether it removes knowledge or only hides it. Evaluations, and what they actually measure. Posts I publish on LessWrong appear here as short stubs with a link. Some posts will be in Russian.

Why I write: practice. Writing and judgment are skills, and both erode when an agent does them for you. Agents are useful to think with. But the line between thinking with one and letting it think instead is thin, and it moves when you are not watching. Judging whether a result is good is still the part of research that has not been made cheap. I would like to keep that skill, and the only way I know is to use it. So I write here and check whether it improves.

The title comes from a Telegram channel I keep for the same reason. A random walk on a line returns to its start with probability 1. In three dimensions it does not, and the dimension of the space you are searching is usually unknown. Pseudorandom is the best I can promise.

Nothing is promised about frequency.
